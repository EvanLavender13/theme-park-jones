# park

This module holds park intent: what the player authored, from which the rest of the park is derived (principle 1, decision 0025). It also makes the game's one refusal, physical validity (principle 5). It is part of tpj_sim and follows its contract (src/sim/SPEC.md).

## Boundary

addParkIntent registers the intent types entrance, path-point, path-segment, box, shop-stock, and opening-hours. Their components are private to this module (decision 0039). addParkEdits registers the fifteen commands. makeParkSchema calls both after the medium's types.

Other modules read intent only through parkEntrances, parkPathPoints, parkSegments, parkCorners, and parkBoxes, the paved surface through parkSurface, with or without walkways, its edges through parkEdges, and its tapers through parkTapers. Each returns every entity holding that kind of intent, in key order, as the public values ParkEntrance, ParkPathPoint, ParkSegment, ParkCorner, ParkBox, ParkEdge, and ParkTaper. Other modules read the opening hours through parkHours, which returns the public value OpeningHours, and ask whether hours are open at a second of the day through isOpenAt. Other modules change intent only through commands.

segmentLine, cornerRadiusFor, arcOf, bendPlace, bendAlong, edgeRadiusFor, stopPosition, stopPositions, pathCrossings, editedSegments, editedSurface, pieceDistance, walkwayPiece, footprintOf, and the sizes pathWidth, MIN_SEGMENT_LENGTH, ARC_TOLERANCE, DEFAULT_CORNER_RADIUS, DEFAULT_EDGE_RADIUS, which is 0, DEFAULT_TAPER_LENGTH, which is 0, DEFAULT_HOURS, which is 9:00 to 21:00, boxSize, ENTRANCE_SIZE, and PARK_SIZE are public, so that the renderer, the tools, and routes measure the same shapes this module validates.

## Intent

The park is a square PARK_SIZE on a side, centered on the origin of the ground.

- A path point has a ground position, a corner radius, and a taper length. The radius is for the corner the point makes when exactly two segments meet there. The taper length is for where the point is a taper (Paved surface).
- A path segment has a kind, guest or backstage, its two path points, From and To, a width, and an edge radius at each end. Segments share a point where the player joined them, and where a path was drawn or moved across them. Routes joins segments only where they share a point (sim/routes/SPEC.md).
- A box has a kind, shop, depot, bench, or toilet block, and a pose.
- A shop has a stock setting: food, drink, or both, which says what it sells. A shop with no stock setting stocks food. parkBoxes gives each box's stock setting, food unless one is set.
- An entrance has a pose.
- The park has opening hours: the second of the day it opens and the second it closes, each less than DAY_SECONDS. If the closing time is at or before the opening time, the hours run past midnight, and if the two are equal, the park is open all day. A park with no hours set has DEFAULT_HOURS, 9:00 to 21:00. parkHours gives the park's hours. isOpenAt(hours, second) says whether a park with the hours is open at that second of the day: from the opening second up to but not including the closing second, past midnight when the hours run past it, and at every second when the park is open all day.

A Pose is a position and a facing on the ground, held as given. A default Pose is at the origin, facing -z.

The save lines hold:

- an entrance's x, z, facing-x, and facing-z;
- a path point's x, z, radius, and taper;
- a path segment's kind, from, to, width, from-edge-radius, and to-edge-radius, from and to being its points' keys;
- a box's kind, x, z, facing-x, and facing-z;
- a shop's stock setting, only when it is drink or both, in a shop-stock line;
- the park's opening and closing seconds, only when they are not DEFAULT_HOURS, in an opening-hours line.

Kinds are saved by name: guest, backstage, shop, depot, bench, and toilet-block. Stock settings are saved by name: food, drink, and both.

## Ground lines

A segment's ground line runs from its From point to its To point, as CarrierPoints (sim/medium/SPEC.md), the first at distance 0 and each at its distance along the line. It is straight, except where it rounds a corner at either end. parkSegments gives each segment with its ground line. segmentLine gives the straight ground line between any two positions. The renderer, the validity check, and routes all use a segment's ground line, so what is drawn, what is refused, and what is walked are the same line.

A segment's ground line is empty when segmentLine between its two points is empty, which it is when either position is not finite or lies outside the park, or the two lie closer together than MIN_SEGMENT_LENGTH. It is also empty when the segment names a key that holds no path point, names the same point twice, or has a width that is not finite and above zero.

A point makes a corner when exactly two segments use it, neither ground line is empty, and their far ends are different points at least MIN_SEGMENT_LENGTH apart. The point must also lie farther than MIN_SEGMENT_LENGTH from the line through the two far ends. Where the segments run straight on, or fold straight back, the point makes no corner. parkCorners gives each corner, in its point's key order. A ParkCorner has the point's key and position, the unit direction along each of its two segments, the lower-keyed segment's first, and the unit direction into the corner, halfway between those two. It also has the radius the corner is rounded with, the largest radius that fits, and where the arc's middle lies.

A corner is rounded with a circular arc that touches both of its segments. The arc's radius is the point's radius, made smaller if needed so that the arc touches each segment no farther from the point than that segment's straight length less what its other end needs, or half the straight length if that is more. The other end needs the farthest of three lengths: how far the arc there would touch the segment with that point's own radius, how far that point's inner paving edges touch it with the radii they hold, and, where that point is a taper whose narrower segment this is and holds a taper length above 0, that length added to how far its inner paving edges touch the segment. If no other segment uses the other end, the arc may use the whole segment. That smaller radius is the largest that fits. If the point's radius is not finite, or is below zero, the corner is sharp. Each of the two segments' ground lines runs straight from its far end to where the arc touches it, then along the arc to the arc's middle, where the two lines meet. Along the arc, the line is made of straight pieces, each within ARC_TOLERANCE of the arc. If the radius is 0, both lines end at the point. Where a point makes no corner, every segment at it ends at the point.

cornerRadiusFor gives the radius whose arc's middle lies a given distance from a corner's point, into the corner. It gives 0 for a distance of 0 or less.

A corner's arc runs from where it touches its first segment, the lower-keyed, to where it touches the second. A place along it is given by a fraction: the place where the arc meets the line from its center through the point that fraction of the way from the first touch to the second. arcOf gives a corner's arc: its center, its radius, and where it touches each segment. bendPlace gives the place for a fraction. bendAlong gives the fraction of the arc's place nearest a ground point, from 0 to 1.

## Paved surface

parkSurface gives the paved surface the park's segments make. It is a list of pieces and a list of edge lines, each belonging to a segment or a point, in ascending key order of what it belongs to. Each piece is a convex polygon on the ground, with its corners counter-clockwise seen from above. Each edge line runs along the surface's outline, with the paving on its left seen from above. A segment with an empty ground line adds nothing. pieceDistance gives how far a point lies from a piece, in meters, and 0 when it lies on the piece.

- A segment's body is its width wide and runs along the straight part of its ground line. At each end it stops where the surface at that point begins.
- At a point that only one segment uses, the body ends square at the point.
- At any other point, the outline turns from each segment's left side, seen walking away from the point, to the right side of the next segment counter-clockwise around the point. Each such turn is an edge. If the next segment lies less than half a turn around, the edge is an inner edge: an arc that touches both sides from outside the paving. If it lies more than half a turn around, the edge is an outer edge: an arc that touches both sides from inside the paving. An edge of radius 0 is sharp: the two sides run straight on until they meet.
- A segment's start at a point is where its ground line's arc touches it, at a corner, and the point itself elsewhere. An inner edge touches each segment between its start and its reach. An outer edge touches each segment no farther from the point than its start or its reach, and no farther behind the point than the wider of the two widths. The radius is the one asked for, made larger or smaller as little as needed to fit. If an inner edge can't touch a segment past its start within its reach, it touches at its reach. If no radius fits, the outline steps straight across at the point, from one side to the other.
- At a corner, the inside edge's radius is the corner's radius less half the narrower width, or 0 if that is less. Every other edge's radius is the edge radius held by the segment it starts from, at that end, or 0 if that is below zero or not finite. A corner's outside edge is one of these, so it curves apart from the corner's radius.
- Where two segments at a point run along one line, the edge between them has no radius. The outline runs around the point from one side to the other at the narrower half width. The wider segment's end steps in to the narrower half width at the point, unless the point is a taper.
- A point is a taper when exactly two segments with ground lines and different widths use it, and on at least one side the outline would step straight across at the point from one segment's side to the other's. On each such side, it curves instead. The narrower segment's side stops its taper length farther from the point than it would stop otherwise, and a curve runs from there to where the wider segment's side begins. The curve is the cubic that leaves the narrower segment's side along it and meets the wider segment's side along it, each direction scaled by how far the curve's far end lies along it, so that it never doubles back. The taper length is the point's taper length, made smaller if needed so that the curve starts no farther from the point than the narrower segment's reach on that side. If the point's taper length is 0, not finite, or below zero, the taper length on each side is twice how far the wider segment's side, where it begins, lies out from the line of the narrower segment's side. Sides that don't step don't change.
- Each side of a segment stops where the edge on that side touches it, or at the point if the edge touches behind the point or steps across it.
- At a corner, the paving between the two segments' bodies belongs to the two segments, divided along the line into the corner. At any other point that two or more segments use, it belongs to the point.
- A segment's reach at one end, on one side, is its straight length less how far the paving at its other end would reach along it on that side if that reach were the whole length, or half its straight length if that is more. That paving reaches to where the edge on that side touches the segment, or, where that side curves at a taper, to where the curve starts. If no other segment uses the other end, the reach is the whole length.
- Along curves, the outline is made of straight pieces, each within ARC_TOLERANCE of its curve.

Edge lines run along each side of each segment, across each square end, and around each point from each segment to the next. They belong to the point they run around. No edge line runs where a body meets a corner's half or a junction. Segments are joined when a chain of shared points links them. Where an edge line lies inside the paving of segments joined to its own, that part of it is hidden. Where segments that aren't joined overlap, each one's edge lines run across the other.

parkSurface can also add walkways. A walkway is a straight strip of paving of a kind and a width, from a door to a place on or inside a segment's paving, joined to that segment. Its end at the door lies along the door's face. If the walkway leaves the face at a shallow angle, with a cosine below 0.1, or if leaning its end along the face would reach past its far end, its door end is square to its line instead. Its other end is square to its line and lies WALKWAY_OVERLAP, 1 cm, past the place it meets, so a walkway that meets a segment's free end covers that end's edge line. Its piece and its edge lines belong to the segment it joins. Edge lines run along both its sides and across its far end. Its door end has none, since the walkway meets its box there. The walkway joins the segment as a segment joined at a point does: the segment's edge lines are hidden where they lie inside the walkway, and the walkway's edge lines are hidden where they lie inside paving joined to that segment. Where a walkway overlaps paving it isn't joined to, each one's edge lines run across the other. If a walkway's segment has no ground line, its width is not above zero, or its door and the place it meets lie closer together than MIN_SEGMENT_LENGTH, it adds nothing. walkwayPiece gives the piece parkSurface adds for one walkway, given the park's segments as parkSegments gives them, or nothing when parkSurface adds nothing for it.

parkEdges gives each edge that has a radius, other than a corner's inside edge, in ascending key order of its point, then of the segment whose left side it starts from. A ParkEdge has the point's key, that segment's key, the unit direction along each of the two segments away from the point, that segment's first, whether it is an inner edge, the radius it is rounded with, and the smallest and largest radii that fit. It also has where its curve's middle lies, where the two sides meet, and the unit direction from there in which the middle moves as the radius grows. edgeRadiusFor gives the radius whose middle lies a given distance from where the sides meet, in that direction. It gives 0 for a distance of 0 or less.

parkTapers gives each taper, in ascending key order of its point. A ParkTaper has the point's key, the place on the narrower segment's line where its sides would stop without curves, the key of the narrower segment, the unit direction along it away from the point, the length of its longest curve, and the longest any of its curves can be.

## Footprints

footprintOf returns the rectangle a pose covers for a size: its width across the facing and its depth along it. It returns none when the pose is not finite or its facing has zero length. Its corners run front left, front right, back right, back left: the front face runs from the first corner to the second, and the back face from the third to the fourth.

## Physical validity

A world is physically valid when no object leaves the park, no two solids overlap, and no segment meets a solid. Solids are the footprints of the entrance and the boxes. Paths occupy the paved surface. A footprint meets a path when it overlaps one of the surface's pieces by more than CONTACT_TOLERANCE. A segment leaves the park when its ground line comes nearer the park's edge than half its width, or when a piece of the surface it or its points own reaches past the park's edge by more than CONTACT_TOLERANCE.

If objects only touch, within CONTACT_TOLERANCE, they never conflict, whatever rounding their facings bring. Segments never conflict with each other, even where they cross or overlap. If an entrance or box has no footprint, or a segment has an empty ground line, the world is invalid. A path point that no segment uses doesn't affect validity. Only a hand-written save can hold such an object. isPhysicallyValid returns whether a world is valid.

A segment's ground line can change when an edit changes a corner at its end, even when the edit leaves the segment's own points in place. A deletion or an insertion can make a corner, remove one, or change how far an arc may reach. The check covers every ground line an edit changes.

## Commands

Fifteen commands change intent: AddPath, AddBox, MoveBox, DeleteSegment, DeleteBox, MovePoint, InsertPoint, RemovePoint, SetCornerRadius, SetSegmentWidth, SetEdgeRadius, SetTaperLength, SetShopStock, SetOpeningHours, and RestoreIntent. No command moves or deletes the entrance. Tools submit commands to the command queue, so they apply between ticks.

AddPath draws a path of one kind through a list of stops, in order. A stop is a new point at a ground position (NewPoint), an existing path point (OnPoint), a place on an existing segment (OnSegment), given as the fraction of the way from the segment's From point to its To point, the point an earlier stop of the same path makes (RepeatStop), as when a path closes a loop on its own start, or a place on one of the path's own earlier segments (OnDrawnSegment), given as the fraction of the way between that segment's two stops. A stop can also be a place on the arc of a corner with a radius above 0 (OnBend), given by the corner's point and a fraction along the arc. stopPositions gives each stop's ground position, and stopPosition gives that of a NewPoint, an OnPoint, or an OnSegment.

MovePoint moves a path point to a target: a new position (NewPoint), another path point (OnPoint), a place on a segment (OnSegment), or a place on an arc (OnBend), given as for AddPath's stops. InsertPoint names a place: a place on a segment (OnSegment) or a place on an arc (OnBend). RemovePoint names a path point. A segment's far end from a point is its other end.

SetCornerRadius names a path point and a radius. SetSegmentWidth names a segment and a width. SetEdgeRadius names a segment, one of its two points, and a radius. SetTaperLength names a path point and a length. SetShopStock names a shop and a stock setting. SetOpeningHours names an opening and a closing second.

RestoreIntent puts intent back as an undo or redo finds it. It names keys to remove, records to set, and records to add. It can also name opening hours to set. A record is a path point's position, radius, and taper length, a segment's kind, two points, width, and edge radii (a SegmentRecord), or a box's kind, pose, and stock setting, each with a key. A record it sets names the path point, segment, or box whose values it replaces. A record it adds uses its key only as a name: a segment that names it as From or To ends at the point added under that name.

On a valid world, a command is accepted exactly when it names what it acts on and the world it describes is physically valid. For AddPath, that means it has at least two stops, each OnPoint names a path point that at least one segment uses and every segment at it has the path's kind, each OnSegment names a segment of the path's kind at a fraction strictly between 0 and 1, each RepeatStop names an earlier stop, each OnDrawnSegment names a segment between two earlier stops at a fraction strictly between 0 and 1, each OnBend names a point that makes a corner whose segments both have the path's kind and whose radius is above 0, at a fraction strictly between 0 and 1, and every segment it adds or splits has a ground line. For MovePoint, it means at least one segment uses the point and all of them have one kind, an OnPoint target names another path point of that kind, an OnSegment target names a segment of that kind that doesn't end at the point, at a fraction strictly between 0 and 1, an OnBend target names such a corner of that kind, neither of whose segments ends at the point, and every segment it moves, splits, or adds has a ground line. For InsertPoint, it means an OnSegment's segment has a ground line and its fraction lies strictly between 0 and 1, or an OnBend names such a corner of either kind, and every part has a ground line. For RemovePoint, it means exactly two segments use the point, their far ends are different points, and the segment it merges has a ground line, as does every part that segment splits into. For SetCornerRadius, it means the key holds a path point and the radius is finite and at least 0. For SetSegmentWidth, it means the key holds a segment with a ground line and the width is finite and above zero. For SetEdgeRadius, it means the key holds a segment with a ground line, the point is one of its two points, and the radius is finite and at least 0. For SetTaperLength, it means the key holds a path point and the length is finite and at least 0. For SetShopStock, it means the key holds a box of kind shop and the setting is food, drink, or both. For SetOpeningHours, it means both seconds are less than DAY_SECONDS. For RestoreIntent, it means each key it removes holds a path point, segment, or box; each record it sets has the key of a path point, segment, or box of the record's type that it doesn't remove; each record it adds has a key that holds no entity and that no other record it adds has; each segment it sets or adds names two different points, each one it adds or a path point it doesn't remove; no segment it leaves in place ends at a point it removes; every number it holds is finite; every radius, edge radius, and taper length is at least 0, and every width is above 0; kinds and stock settings are known; the hours it sets, if any, have both seconds less than DAY_SECONDS, and no record it adds holds hours; and every box it sets or adds has a footprint that stays in the park and conflicts with no other solid and no piece of the surface it leaves. For every command, the world it describes is physically valid when every ground line the command adds or changes, including one whose corner it changes, is not empty and stays in the park, and no piece of the surface the command adds or changes meets a solid or leaves the park. So a DeleteSegment, RemovePoint, or InsertPoint is refused if a corner it changes would round into a solid. isAccepted returns whether a command would be accepted. If the command is not accepted, applyCommand changes nothing. A ghost shown as valid always matches what its commit does.

Every point a command other than RestoreIntent adds has DEFAULT_TAPER_LENGTH. If a command is accepted, it leaves exactly the intent it describes and changes nothing else:

- AddPath adds a point at each NewPoint, OnSegment, OnBend, and OnDrawnSegment stop, with DEFAULT_CORNER_RADIUS. A RepeatStop uses its earlier stop's point. It adds a segment from each stop's point to the next, of the path's kind and pathWidth, with DEFAULT_EDGE_RADIUS at both ends. Each segment that OnSegment stops name splits at their points, in order of fraction. The segment keeps its key, kind, width, and edge radius at its From end, and now ends at its first split point, with DEFAULT_EDGE_RADIUS there. A new segment of the same kind and width runs on from each split point to the next, and the last ends at the segment's old To point, with the segment's old edge radius there. Each other end of a new part has DEFAULT_EDGE_RADIUS. Each of the path's own segments that OnDrawnSegment stops name runs through their points in order of fraction, as a run of segments.
- AddBox adds one box.
- MoveBox replaces a box's pose and keeps its key, kind, and stock setting.
- DeleteSegment destroys the segment, and each of its points that no other segment uses.
- DeleteBox destroys the box.
- MovePoint to a NewPoint or OnSegment target moves the point there and keeps its key, radius, and taper length. Every segment at it follows and keeps its key, kind, width, and edge radii. An OnSegment target's segment splits at the point, as for an OnSegment stop.
- MovePoint to an OnPoint target destroys the moved point. Every segment at it ends at the target point instead and keeps its key, kind, width, and edge radii. A segment between the two points is destroyed. If no segment then uses the target point, it is destroyed too.
- InsertPoint adds a point at the place, with DEFAULT_CORNER_RADIUS, and the segment or corner splits there, as for an OnSegment or OnBend stop.
- RemovePoint destroys the point and merges its two segments. The one with the lower key keeps its key, kind, and far end, and takes the wider of the two widths. Its end at the point moves to the other segment's far end, with the other segment's edge radius there, and the other segment is destroyed.
- A corner splits at places on its arc, given by OnBend stops, an OnBend target or insert, or where a segment meets the arc. A point is added at each place, with DEFAULT_CORNER_RADIUS. The corner's point moves to where the line touching the arc at the first place meets the first segment's line. A new point is added where the lines touching the arc at each two places in a row meet, and where the line touching it at the last place meets the second segment's line. Each of these corner points takes the radius the corner was rounded with. The second segment's end moves from the corner's point to the last of them. New segments run from the corner's point through the places and the points between them to that last point. The last new segment takes the second segment's width, and the rest the first's. Each new corner's outside edge takes the old corner's outside edge radius, and every other new end has DEFAULT_EDGE_RADIUS. The new corners' arcs together lie exactly on the old arc.
- SetCornerRadius sets the point's radius, as given, and changes nothing else. A radius larger than fits is kept, and the corner is rounded with the largest that fits.
- SetSegmentWidth sets the segment's width, as given, and changes nothing else.
- SetEdgeRadius sets the segment's edge radius at the point, as given, and changes nothing else. A radius that doesn't fit is kept, and the edge is rounded with the nearest that fits.
- SetTaperLength sets the point's taper length, as given, and changes nothing else. A length longer than fits is kept, and each curve runs the longest that fits.
- SetShopStock sets the shop's stock setting and changes nothing else. A shop set to food holds no shop-stock line, as if none had been set.
- SetOpeningHours sets the park's opening hours and changes nothing else. A park set to DEFAULT_HOURS holds no opening-hours line, as if none had been set.
- RestoreIntent destroys each path point, segment, and box it removes. It sets each record it sets on the path point, segment, or box with that key, which keeps its key. It adds each record it adds with a key from the counter, in the order it lists them: path points first, then segments, then boxes. No join, split, or curve rule applies, so it leaves exactly the records it gives. If it names opening hours, it sets them last, as SetOpeningHours does.

Where a segment that AddPath adds, MovePoint moves, or RemovePoint merges meets another segment of its kind, the two join. The other segment can be one the command leaves in place, or another one the command adds or moves.

- If each end of each segment lies within MIN_SEGMENT_LENGTH of the line through the other, the segments lie along one line. They don't meet, and neither splits.
- Otherwise, if an end of one segment lies within MIN_SEGMENT_LENGTH of the other segment, and farther than MIN_SEGMENT_LENGTH from both of the other's ends, the other segment splits at that end's point.
- Otherwise, if the segments cross, the command adds a point at the crossing, with DEFAULT_CORNER_RADIUS, and both split there. If the crossing lies within MIN_SEGMENT_LENGTH of a point the command already places, as where a path doubles back across a segment, both split at that point instead.

If they meet within MIN_SEGMENT_LENGTH of an end of each, nothing splits. A segment that meets several others splits at each of their points, in order along it, together with any OnSegment or OnDrawnSegment stops or OnSegment target on it, as described above. pathCrossings gives the ground position of each point where AddPath, MovePoint, or RemovePoint splits a segment this way, other than at an OnSegment or OnDrawnSegment stop or a MovePoint's OnSegment target. It gives none if the world refuses the command.

A segment the command leaves in place meets along the straight part of its ground line, between where the arcs at its ends touch it. A corner the command leaves in place, with both of its segments, meets along its arc, unless the command joins the corner's own point, as with an OnPoint stop or target. Where a segment the command adds, moves, or merges crosses an arc or ends on it, the corner splits there. If that place lies within MIN_SEGMENT_LENGTH of where the arc touches a segment, that segment splits where the arc touches it instead. A segment the command adds, moves, or merges meets others along the straight line between its points.

Joins and splits keep every curve. When an edit leaves a point that made a corner with three or more segments, the edge radius held for the corner's inside edge becomes the corner's radius less half the narrower width, or 0 if that is less, and the point keeps its radius. When an edit leaves a point that had two or more segments with one, its radius becomes DEFAULT_CORNER_RADIUS. When AddPath or MovePoint, or a split, adds segment ends at a point that already had two or more segments, each run of them lands inside one edge. The edges on either side of the run touch that edge's two segments where it did, so its curve stays where it was on both sides. Edges between the added ends take its radius, and a sharp edge stays sharp. When an edit removes segment ends from a point that keeps two or more and stays where it was, each edge that now spans a gap touches the segment it starts from where that segment's old edge did, so a curve a join cut in two comes back when the join goes. A merged segment's end keeps the edge radius RemovePoint gives it. When an edit leaves a point that had three or more segments with exactly two that make a corner, the point's radius becomes its inside edge's radius plus half the narrower width. If that edge's radius is 0, the point's radius becomes the smaller of its own radius and half the narrower width.

editedSegments gives each segment an edit adds, or whose ground line or width it changes, as it would lie after the edit, in ascending key order. A new segment has the key the commit would give it. The parts of a segment the edit splits are among them, and so is a segment whose corner the edit changes. It gives them whether or not the world refuses the edit for meeting a solid. It gives none when the edit doesn't name what it acts on, when a segment the edit adds or reshapes has no ground line, for edits of boxes and of the opening hours, and for RestoreIntent, which shows no ghost.

editedSurface gives the pieces and edge lines of each segment and point whose part of the surface an edit changes, as they would lie after the edit, in ascending key order of the segment or point they belong to. It gives none when the edit doesn't name what it acts on, when a segment the edit adds or reshapes has no ground line, for edits of boxes and of the opening hours, and for RestoreIntent, which shows no ghost.

Everything a command adds takes a key from the key counter. A pose or position is kept exactly as given.

A ParkEdit holds any one of the fifteen commands. It is the value a tool shows as a ghost and commits, or the RestoreIntent an undo or redo commits. isAccepted on a ParkEdit is isAccepted on its command. queueEdit pushes the command, so a cycle applies it as if it were pushed directly.

## The new park

makeNewPark(schema, seed) returns a physically valid world at tick 0, pending resolution. The world holds an entrance with its back on the park's edge, facing in, and a short guest path in front of it: two path points and the segment between them. tests/parks/new.park is the save of makeNewPark(seed) with seed 1.
