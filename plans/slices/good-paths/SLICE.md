# Slice: Good Paths

Status: complete

## Summary

The player draws paths that look and behave the way they meant. A path is a network of shared points joined by straight segments. Each segment has its own width, and each corner has its own radius. The player reshapes any path after drawing it by dragging, inserting, and deleting points, by dragging a corner's radius, and by dragging a segment's side to set its width. Every snap shows a guide, and one held key suppresses them all. Joined paths draw as one paved surface with rounded inside corners and an edge line along its outline. Shops placed beside a path face it and line up along it. Guests walk on the paving like people, across a path's whole width and straight across it to where they are going, instead of filing along its center line. Undo and redo cover every edit. This is the right next goal because building is why this game exists (docs/vision.md), and Planet Coaster 2 shows what bad paths cost. Its paths fuse by nearness, its seams show, its delete removes too much, its snaps ignore the off switch, and its guests walk single file. Plazas and open paving wait for a later slice, which can build on paths that already work.

## End-to-end scenario

The player starts a new park and lays out a main street. They pick the guest path tool, click the end of the short path in front of the entrance, then click 60 m straight ahead. A dashed guide shows the run is straight on from the entrance path, and the length reads 60 m exactly because it snapped to whole meters. They drag the segment's side out until it is 10 m wide.

From the far end they draw a side path at 90° to the street, 3 m wide, and turn it 45° partway along. A guide marks each angle as it snaps. The bend gets a default corner radius, and they drag its radius handle until the bend is a wide, smooth arc. They draw a second side path from the middle of the street. Clicking on the street's side snaps onto it, a marker shows the join, and the street splits there at a new shared point. They draw a loop path across the first side path. The ghost marks the crossing as a new junction before they commit. Once it is committed, the two paths meet at a shared point.

Where paths meet, the paving reads as one surface. The inside corners are sharp until the player curves them with their handles. A thin edge line runs along the outline of the whole surface and never across a join. Each path's free end is cut square.

Next they adjust the layout. They drag the shared point where three paths meet, and every segment there follows. They click a segment to insert a point and pull it out to bend the path. They delete a point where a path kinked, and its two segments merge into one straight segment. They delete one segment of the loop, and only that segment goes. To put a point where no snap will let it go, they hold the suppress key and drag freely, and every guide disappears while they hold it. One undo puts back the deleted segment, and redo removes it again.

They place shops along both sides of the main street. As each ghost nears the street, it turns to face it, slides to a fixed setback from the paving's edge, and connects by its front door. They draw a backstage path behind one row, past the shops' back doors, and place a depot beside it. The depot turns to face the backstage path and connects by its front door. Where the backstage path crosses a side path, the two paths don't join.

Guests arrive. They walk on the paving itself, not along the paths' center lines. On the 10 m main street they spread across its width, each at its own pace, those going one way keeping to one side, and they step around one another. A guest heading for a shop across the street walks straight to its door. On the 3 m side paths they walk close together, and at a bend they keep to its inside without leaving the paving. When the player widens, splits, or merges a segment under walking guests, the guests keep walking from where they stand. Nobody jumps along the path.

## Medium

The slice adds one kind of field, a ground field, and one field of that kind, guest presence, so guests can step around one another through the medium (principle 3). It adds no flows. It changes three things that cross capabilities: park intent, the network type that every field and flow lives on, and how places carry over when a network is derived again. Abbreviations: EB is effortless-building, NN is navigable-networks, SM is shared-medium, BG is believable-guests, LS is legible-simulation, and PO is plausible-operations.

- Networks: the guest and backstage networks, in SM's network type, produced by NN. Each segment is a carrier, keyed by the segment's key and following its ground line, as each path is today. Nodes sit only at points, shared or free, and at both ends of each connector: its door, and where it meets a segment. Lines that cross or come near each other without sharing a point don't join, so routes no longer derives junctions from geometry. Every carrier now has a walkable width. A segment's carrier has the segment's width, and a connector has its kind's walkway width, which is today's fixed path width. SM's network type answers the width and direction at a place. Consumers: BG takes each guest's route from the network and works out each guest's place from where it stands on the paving. PO, every field, LS's overlay, and NN's own graph view are unchanged in how they read networks.
- Shaping points: a shared point where exactly two segments meet, with no door's connector, is a node only so the line can bend. If guests chose there, the number of points the player drew would change how guests behave. BG consumes this: a guest walks straight through a node with exactly two edges and no anchor, without choosing there. It still chooses once for every other node, a little before reaching it, and at the triggers it chooses at today.
- Ground fields: a new kind of field in SM's medium, behind the common sampling interface (decision 0005). Its entries are keyed by entity and sit at ground positions, each with a value its producer sets, and it is kept from tick to tick as kept fields are (decision 0031). A producer sets or drops its entries as it steps, and an entity's entry is dropped when the entity is gone, so sampling only ever finds entities that are in the park. Sampling it at a ground point gives the entries within a radius of that point, in key order. That radius is a straight-line measure, added to the medium's short list of them: it measures how near bodies are, not how far one travels, so route distance stays the only measure of travel (principle 4). Produced by SM as a type, as fields on networks are. Consumer: BG's guest presence. Ground fields are SM's deepening candidate of that name, drawn into this slice because a mechanic now needs one.
- Guest presence: a ground field BG produces and samples. Each guest's entry sits where it stood at the end of the last tick and holds which way and how fast it was walking. A guest that leaves the park drops its entry. Each guest samples it around itself to choose its sidestep, so one guest affects another's walking only through the medium, and nothing reads guests' state directly.
- Carry-over: SM's carryOver moves a holder's place to the new network. Today, if a carrier changes shape, a place moves to the nearest place on the same carrier. On a split, that would move a guest on the split-off half to the split point. Under this slice, if an edit leaves ground where it was under a different key, a place on that ground stays at the same ground position. That ground can be either half of a split segment, or two segments merged into one. Every holder of places uses it, including hungry footfall, which already carries its entries over with carryOver. Guests hold a ground position instead (see Walking on the paving), so a guest stays where it stood whenever paving is still there.
- Connectors: produced by NN. A door connects if it is within reach of the drawn paving of a segment of its kind, not of its center line. EB's frontage setback must stay inside that reach, so a box placed by frontage always connects.
- Route distance: unchanged in kind, produced by NN. It is measured along segment center lines and the arcs of their rounded corners (principle 4). Width doesn't change route distance or capacity.

Dependencies that are not fields or flows, ruled on by decision 0025:

- Park intent: points and segments, authored by EB. A point is a ground position. A point with exactly two segments has a corner radius, and a point can have a taper length. A segment has a kind, guest or backstage, its two points, a width, and an edge radius at each end. Points are shared only by segments of one kind. EB adds edit commands to add a path, move a point, insert a point into a segment, remove a point, delete a segment, set a segment's width, set a corner's radius, set an edge radius, set a taper length, and restore intent for undo and redo. Two rules hold for every edit. If an edit ends a point on a point or segment of its kind at a visible snap, they join there. If an edit makes segments of one kind cross, they split at the crossing and share a point there. The ghost marks each such join before commit. Nothing else joins. Removing a point is accepted only where exactly two segments use it, and merges them into one segment with the wider of their widths. Deleting a segment deletes only that segment. A point left with no segments is removed. sim/park publishes each segment with its key, kind, width, ground line, and the keys of its two points, so NN finds shared points from intent, not from geometry. Segments that share a point have ground lines that meet there. At a rounded corner, they meet at the middle of the arc.
- Candidate resolution: every new edit previews on a candidate world, as placements do today. Undo and redo commit without a preview (decision 0029).
- Walking on the paving: BG reads the guest paving's pieces from sim/park's parkSurface, and each guest door's walkway piece with the key of its connector, as the ground guests walk on. sim/park and routes don't publish a walkway's piece with its connector key today, so member 6 adds that to them: sim/park gives a walkway's piece on its own, built by the code parkSurface uses, and routes gives each walkway with its connector's key. A guest holds a ground position on that paving, saved with it. A guest makes its choice for each node a little before it reaches it, about 10 m ahead, so its route always runs past the next node. Each tick it aims at the farthest point along that route that a straight line from it reaches without leaving the paving, moved across the path to the guest's own side by its own share of the width and kept on the paving. It walks toward that aim and steps around nearby guests, which it senses only through guest presence. On wide paving this walks it straight down its own side, and straight to a door across the street. Decision 0038 records that walking anywhere on the route's paving travels that route (principle 4). A guest stays inside the outline of the paving it is on: the visible edge lines of its own joined paving, and each walkway's end at its door. Other paths' edge lines don't bound it, so where unjoined paths overlap it stays on its own path. Its place on the network is where it has got to along its route, the point of its route nearest where it stands, so every field, flow, and route distance it reads or feeds works as before. After an edit, its place is the nearest place on the carrier of the segment, point, or walkway it stands on. After an edit, a guest keeps its ground position if paving is still there, and otherwise moves to the nearest paving. Other guests sense a guest's position and motion only through guest presence, and tooling reads its inspection record. Nothing else reads a guest's ground position.
- Inspection records: unchanged in kind. A guest's record already gives its ground position, which is now where it stands on the paving, so drawing and picking read it as before.

The paved surface, its rounded inside corners, and its edge line are EB's, published by sim/park as park intent's derived shape (decision 0025). NN reads the surface's pieces to measure each door's reach, so a change to the paving changes which doors connect. EB's tools read it to front boxes. LS's food overlay shades the guest paving's pieces, so the overlay covers each path at its drawn width. BG walks guests on the guest paving's pieces, so guests walk exactly where the paths are drawn. Where segments of one kind overlap without sharing a point, each one's edge line is drawn across the other, so unjoined paving never looks joined. The ghost shows this before commit. Undo history is the tools' session state: a list of edits and their inverses. It is not intent and is never saved (principle 1). If undo restores something deleted, the restored thing gets a fresh key from the counter, so keys are still never reused (src/sim/SPEC.md).

## Members

These are in build order. Milestone slugs are provisional until each capability plans them.

1. `shared-medium/carrier-widths`: every carrier has a width that construction, copies, and loads keep, with queries for the width and direction at a place, and carryOver keeps a place at its ground position when its ground survives under a different key. Until member 3, routes gives each carrier its kind's current fixed width. Depends on: none.
2. `effortless-building/path-network`: paths as points and segments. This member covers every edit command in Medium, the draw tool and its snaps, guides, length readout, and suppress key, and editing handles for points, corners, and widths. It also covers undo and redo, the frontage snap, validity that tests boxes against the paved surface as it is drawn, rounded corners included, so what is drawn and what is refused stay the same shape, and the paved surface drawn as one surface. It rewrites every checked-in park, the new park template, and the scenarios' park builders, and adds main-street.park. The world checks' chosen edits include each new edit command. It updates every reader of parkPaths and groundLine to the published segments in the same change, without changing what those readers do. Depends on: member 1.
3. `navigable-networks/wide-routes`: nodes come only from points and connectors' ends, each carrier takes its segment's width, doors connect within reach of a segment's edge, and the graph view shows the new networks. Depends on: members 1 and 2.
4. `legible-simulation/wide-overlay`: the food overlay shades the guest paving itself, across the whole width of every segment and junction, instead of a band of ground beside each path, so wide paths never hide it. Depends on: member 3.
5. `shared-medium/ground-fields`: ground fields as the Medium describes them, with their sampling, setting and dropping entries while stepping, dropping a gone entity's entry, keeping across ticks, copies, saves, and the world checks' coverage once a park file holds one. Depends on: none.
6. `believable-guests/spread-guests`: guests walk on the paving with a ground position of their own, each at its own speed. Each chooses its next turn a little before it reaches a node, aims at the farthest point along its network route it can reach in a straight line without leaving the paving, moved to its own side of the path, and steps around nearby guests, sensed through the guest presence field. Its place on the network follows from where it stands. Guests walk straight through shaping points without choosing, and still choose once for every other node and at today's triggers. tpj_scenarios --slice-parks makes warm-main-street.park. It also adds the walkway pieces described under Walking on the paving to sim/park and routes. Guest presence is the first ground field. Before guests walk on the paving, it checks once, over every park in tests/parks and tests/parks/stress, that the outline guests walk inside closes. Depends on: members 3 and 5.

Simulation output changes on purpose, because route lengths change from today's smooth curves to straight segments and arcs. The runtime report taken after the last member becomes the new baseline. Old saves don't load.

## Out of scope

- Plazas, areas, clearings, stamps, and open paving. These are the next slice.
- Curved segments other than rounded corners, and Bezier handles.
- Width that varies along one segment.
- Box-select of several points, and dragging a whole segment.
- Boxes that follow a path when it moves. If a moved path would meet a box, the ghost shows the edit refused.
- A tool that lays rows of boxes.
- Path materials, textures, and decoration.
- Terrain, slopes, stairs, bridges, and heights.
- Queue paths and one-way paths.
- Width as capacity, crowding, lanes, and queues that form in the simulation. Guests only step around one another.
- Route distance measured across the paving. Guests choose by network route distance, which on wide paving is a little longer than the walk they take.
- Restoring a deleted thing's old key on undo.
- Moving the entrance.
- Player-facing UI. ImGui stands in, and the question stays open (docs/open-questions.md).

## Research notes

- Planet Coaster 2's problems come from one merged surface: fusing by nearness, seams, delete overreach, and a snap the off switch misses. Here intent is points and segments, joins happen only at visible snaps and marked crossings, and the surface is derived.
- Snaps feel right when their reach is in screen pixels, the smallest correction wins, a snap releases a little farther out than it caught, every satisfied guide is drawn, and one key turns them all off (SketchUp, Figma, tldraw).
- Cities: Skylines splits a road where another crosses it and removes points left with nothing. Neither it nor its sequel can merge two segments at a point or undo without mods.
- Build the surface at each point the way road games build intersections. Each piece marks which of its edges are outline, and edge strips are drawn along those edges over the fills, so joins show no line and unjoined overlaps do (plans/effortless-building/path-network/RESEARCH.md). Clipper2's offset-and-back rounding was rejected because it also joins nearby separate paths.
- Guests drawn beside a center line can't stay on the paving at junctions, since the lines and the paving are different shapes there. Guests walk on the paving itself instead.
- Guests walk inside the paving's outline, its visible edge lines, which close in every park. The pieces under it can't serve as a walkable mesh: a short segment inside a wider junction stacks its pieces on the junction's, and slivers and seams leave edges that don't pair (plans/believable-guests/spread-guests/RESEARCH.md).
- Guests string-pull along their network route across the paving, as Detour's path corridor does, and sidestep one another by predicting their closest approach, decided from the last tick's positions so the result doesn't depend on order. They sense each other through a ground field in the shared medium, since its existing fields sit at network places.

Depth is in RESEARCH.md.
