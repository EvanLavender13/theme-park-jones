# Slice: Paths and Plazas

Status: planned

## Summary

The player lays out a park's walkable ground as layered shapes, the way an art director sketches: paths drawn as lines of any width, areas clicked out as polygons or stamped down as squares, and clearing shapes that cut holes, all stacked newest on top and all edited the same way, by dragging, inserting, and deleting vertices, rounding corners, and dragging a path's side to set its width. There is no plaza object. A plaza is paving wide enough to cross, and the walkable graph is derived from the paved ground's shape alone, by spreading points evenly over it and linking neighbors, so a narrow path becomes one line down its middle, a wide path two lanes, and open paving an even mesh guests cross directly. It is the right next goal because building is this game's reason to exist (docs/vision.md), the boxes-and-tubes tools were the minimum to draw a park, and Planet Coaster 2 shows how much a finicky path system costs: its paths and stamps fuse into one hidden surface that shows when something is deleted, its snaps act when switched off, and its guests stall on plazas. Here every piece of paving stays its own shape, the merged ground is derived every time and never saved (principle 1), every snap is visible and suppressible, and nothing about routing depends on how the ground was drawn.

## End-to-end scenario

The player builds a grand entrance modeled on Kings Dominion's. From the entrance they pick the area tool and click out a long rectangle for a promenade, 45 m wide. Guides appear as they click: each edge snaps straight on from the last, and to 90 and 45 degrees from it, with its length read out in meters and snapped to whole meters. Holding a key suppresses every snap at once. Each snap shows as a guide line in the ghost, and none acts without one.

Down the promenade's middle they draw a clearing shape for a reflecting pool. The paving under it disappears, and the promenade reads as two broad walks, one on each side of the pool, joined at both ends. At the promenade's far end they stamp a square, size and turn it as it lands, and drag one corner's radius handle until all four round fully into a circle. They draw two guest paths crossing through its middle, and stamp four small squares between the arms, rounded a little, as clearings for planters. The circle, the cross, and the promenade read as one paved surface, with no seams where they overlap. Narrow paths leave the hub to either side.

They click an edge of the promenade to insert a vertex and drag it out to widen one corner, then drag a path's side to widen it from 3 m to 5 m. They continue a narrow path from its end, delete a vertex that made a kink, and drag a whole planter clearing a little off center. They box-select the ends of three paths where they meet and drag the meeting point, and all three follow. A shape covered by others is reached by clicking again at the same spot, which cycles down through the stack. They bring a planter clearing to the front after a path was drawn over it. A step they dislike goes with one undo and comes back with redo.

They place shops along both sides of the promenade. Each ghost turns to face the paving, slides to a setback from its edge, and connects by its front door. A depot goes behind the shops on one side, with a backstage path drawn to their back doors and on around the promenade's end to the back doors of the shops on the other side. The backstage path crosses a guest path without joining it.

Guests arrive. On the narrow paths they walk in a line down the middle, spread across its width. On each side of the pool they walk the broad walk in lanes. In the hub they cross between the arms around the planters. Wanderers on open paving keep their heading rather than jittering. A guest no longer weighs its options at every node, since open paving is all nodes: it decides what to go for after each stretch of walking, wherever it is, and at once when its target's offer ends or its route is cut, and between decisions it simply steps toward its target or keeps wandering. The graph view shows the points spread over the ground and their links: one line down each narrow path, two rows where a path is twice as wide, an even mesh over open paving, and nothing over the pool or the planters.

The player deletes the pool. The promenade becomes one open area, and guests start crossing straight between the shops facing each other across it. Only the tiles of ground around the deleted pool re-derive their graph. Guests already on the promenade are carried to where they stand, and nobody is lost or stuck. Undo brings the pool back.

## Acceptance criteria

Two park files are checked in, both with guests in the park after a warmup. tests/parks/pool-promenade.park holds the scenario's park: the entrance, the promenade area with the pool clearing, shops along both sides, the round hub with its crossing paths and four planter clearings, narrow paths leaving the hub, a depot, and a backstage path to every shop's back door. tests/parks/open-promenade.park is the same park with the pool clearing deleted. tpj_scenarios --slice-parks regenerates both alongside the other slices' parks. The boxes-and-tubes slice's parks are regenerated with their paths as path shapes.

1. In both parks, route distance between any two places of the guest network that lie on the paved ground is never less than the shortest walk between their ground points inside the guest paved ground, and at most 10% more than it plus the widest point spacing. (integration test)
2. Every link of each network other than a door's connector lies on the paved ground of its kind, so no route crosses a clearing, the pool, or unpaved ground, and guest and backstage networks never share a node, even where their shapes overlap. (integration test)
3. A path shape of a standard width, with nothing overlapping it, derives a single row of links down its middle: every point along it has two links, except at its ends, where other ground meets it, and where a door connects. (integration test)
4. Intent whose shapes stack to the same paved ground derives the same networks: one area, and several overlapping shapes covering exactly that ground, give equal networks. (integration test)
5. An edit leaves every carrier in a tile whose ground, within the tile's margin, it did not change unchanged, in key and points, so guests and hungry footfall there are untouched. ## Replaced behavior and migration

The slice replaces how paths are saved, edited, drawn, and turned into networks, so much of what exists today changes with it. Each member below owns the part in its modules, and plans it as tasks of its own milestone, so none of it surfaces during implementation.

Two kinds of test change follow, and both go through each member's separate test pass, never through the context that writes the code. Tests that only build parks, through AddPath, DeletePath, or park files, and assert on something the slice keeps, have their setup rewritten in the new commands with their assertions unchanged. Tests that assert behavior the slice replaces are removed with the spec text they check, and the member's test pass writes their replacements from the new spec.

Old saves are not loaded. There are no players' parks to keep, so every checked-in park is rewritten in the new format by layered-paving and the old path line is refused. Parks that hold guests or footfall (fed, warm, and cut, and the stress parks full.park and winding-path.park) are regenerated again by each later member that changes what they hold: ground-becomes-routes re-keys every guest's carrier, and open-ground-guests adds to what a guest saves. tpj_scenarios --compare and the runtime report's before and after comparisons change on purpose across the members that change the networks, and a fresh runtime report on every stress park, taken after ground-becomes-routes and open-ground-guests, is the new baseline.

## Medium

The slice adds no fields or flows. What crosses capabilities is park intent, the paved ground derived from it, and the network type every field and flow lives on. Abbreviations are as in the boxes-and-tubes slice, with LS for legible-simulation.

- Networks: the guest and backstage graphs, now derived from each kind's paved ground rather than from path lines, with a walkable width at every place of every carrier. Points spread over the ground, closer together where it is narrow and farther apart where it is open, up to a widest spacing, are linked to their neighbors where the link lies on the ground, and chains of points with two links become one carrier, so a narrow path is a few long carriers and open paving a mesh of short ones. The points are derived tile by tile over fixed tiles of the park. Where ground crosses a tile border, fixed border points derived from the ground near the border alone pin it, and each tile relaxes its own points with its border points held, from the ground within a fixed margin of it alone. Links join points of one tile, border points belonging to both tiles they lie between, so carriers end at border points and never cross a border. The networks therefore remain a function of intent alone, and an edit re-derives only the tiles whose ground within their margin it changed. A carrier's key derives from its points, so carriers in untouched tiles keep their keys. This supersedes navigable-networks' and shared-medium's statements that carriers are keyed from intent: a place in a re-derived tile whose carrier changed is retired, so guests there are carried to the nearest place of the network as the existing rules already do, and hungry footfall held there starts again. Members 2 and 4 update those capabilities' wording when they are planned. A carrier's width at a place is the clear width of paving around it. A door's reach is measured to the edge of its kind's paved ground. The width, and queries for the width and direction at a place, are added to SM's network type. NN produces the networks. Consumed by BG (movement, wandering, and the spread guests are drawn with), by PO (anchors, unchanged), by every field sampled on network places, by LS (the overlay), and by EB (walkways and the graph view).
- Route distance: unchanged, measured along the new carriers, so crossing open paving costs the length of the links walked, never less than the straight walk (principle 4). Produced by NN.

Dependencies that are not fields or flows, ruled on by decision 0025:

- Park intent: paving shapes by kind, guest or backstage, each a path (a line of points with one width), an area (a polygon of corners with a radius at each), or a clearing (an area that clears paving of both kinds), with a stacking order, plus boxes as before. A stamp is an area once placed. Authored by EB through new edit commands: add, move, and delete shapes; move, insert, and delete vertices; set a corner's radius and a path's width; move several vertices of one or more shapes at once; bring a shape to the front; continue a path from its end. Nothing is ever merged or rewritten in intent except by the edit the player made.
- Paved ground: each kind's ground as stacked shapes give it, derived by EB from intent every time, and read through a public query in sim/park, as ground lines are today. NN derives the networks from it, EB renders it and checks boxes against it, and LS shades it.
- Candidate resolution: every new edit previews on a candidate world as placements do today.
- Inspection records: unchanged. BG's guest mesh reads a guest's place from its record and the walkable width and direction there from the network's public queries.

Undo history is the tools' own session state, a list of edits and their inverses. It is not intent and is never saved (principle 1). Undo and redo commit at once, without a preview (decision 0029). Undoing a deletion recreates the deleted shape under its original key, a key the counter issued and no entity now holds. Keys are still never handed to a different entity.

## Out of scope

Splitting and joining paths. Smooth curves through area vertices and Bezier bend handles on edges. Width varying along one path; a flare is another shape. Shared vertices between shapes. Stamps beyond a square, and saved or user-made stamps. Path materials, textures, and brush-painted ground. Frontage saved as an attachment, so boxes follow a reshaped edge. A tool that lays rows of boxes. Terrain, slopes, stairs, bridges, and heights. Queue paths and one-way paths. Crowding, capacity from width, and crowd avoidance in the simulation. Guests' local knowledge of the park and wayfinding with maps (a believable-guests deepening). Boxes standing inside paving, such as kiosks, and supplying boxes from inside guest areas, which waits on how supplies and staff reach guest areas (docs/open-questions.md). Moving the entrance. Player-facing UI: ImGui stands in, and the question stays open (docs/open-questions.md).

## Open questions

- Point spacing, how it widens on open ground, and the route distance tolerance. The criteria assume 10% plus the widest spacing. Resolved by measuring both parks and a large synthetic ground against a reference shortest walk in ground-becomes-routes; Evan decides if the measured error calls for a different tolerance.
- The tile size and margin, and whether re-deriving an edit's tiles is fast enough to resolve a candidate on every pointer move, as previews do. Resolved by measuring in ground-becomes-routes; smaller tiles or fewer relaxation rounds are the fallback, and preview must still equal commit (principle 8).
- Whether a park with plazas holds its tick and its previews within budget. Resolved by the plaza stress park's runtime report in ground-becomes-routes. Wider spacing on open ground, coarser footfall, and hierarchical routing are the fallbacks, and Evan chooses among them with the numbers in hand.
- Snap reach, guide spacing, setback, the stamp's default size, and the re-choose distance are tuning values, set while planning layered-paving, ground-becomes-routes, and open-ground-guests and adjusted by drawing parks.

## Research notes

- Platter gives each building its own frame snapped to the road edge with a setback, which is what makes curved streets work; the frontage snap follows it.
- Planet Coaster 2's fusing, seams, and lost strokes come from saving merged results, its hidden snaps from snaps the toggle misses, and its stalled guests from plazas with no structure; intent here stays the shapes, the ground is derived, and every snap is visible.
- Width belongs on the path, never as parallel paths (Parkitect's distance inflation); plazas are paving, never tiled path pieces.
- Cities: Skylines 2's surfaces are node polygons players layer by disabling the overlap check, and Figma gives each vertex its own corner radius; paving is layered shapes with straight edges and corner radii, and holes are clearing shapes that stay objects.
- Cities: Skylines keeps every piece of surface owned by one node or segment, and its middle nodes show why shaping vertices must never be routing nodes.
- The graph comes from the paved ground alone: Lloyd's relaxation or DistMesh spreads points evenly, settling into one row on a narrow path and an even fill on open ground, and Delaunay neighbors on the ground link them; a medial axis, portals, a width threshold, and a uniform grid were rejected.

Depth is in RESEARCH.md.
