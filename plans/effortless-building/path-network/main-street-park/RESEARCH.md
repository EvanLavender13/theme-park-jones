# Research: main-street-park

## How should the main street park be laid out so it reads well?

Disney's Main Street, U.S.A. is the model the slice's scenario follows. Three of its rules suit a park made of paths and boxes:

- The entrance opens onto the street like the doors of a theater. The visitor passes through a narrow gate and the street opens out ahead.
- Unbroken rows of shops line both sides of the street, all set back the same distance, so the street reads as one long view.
- The street ends at a hub, a junction with paths leading out to the rest of the park. In Disney's parks, a landmark at the far end draws visitors along the street.

Park-building players give similar advice for Planet Coaster: main paths 8 to 10 m wide, side paths narrower, and loops so that guests keep moving instead of turning back.

For this park, these findings suggest the following:

- The 3 m entrance path widens into the 10 m street along a long taper, so the street opens out like a theater.
- Shops stand in even rows along both sides of the street, each at the frontage setback, and gaps appear only where a side path leaves the street.
- The street ends in a T, and the side paths run out from it. The loop is a rounded square that the east side path passes through, so the ghost marks two crossings.
- Rounded corners use concentric outside edges, so each bend reads as a curve of even width.
- The backstage path runs behind both rows and around the street's far end, with the depot centered behind the T. Each place it crosses a guest path shows the two kinds stay separate.

Rejected:

- A round hub with spokes at the street's end. It needs either open paving or a ring that crosses the street, and plazas wait for the next slice.
- Shops along the side paths. They would need their own backstage paths, and the scenario places shops only along the street.
