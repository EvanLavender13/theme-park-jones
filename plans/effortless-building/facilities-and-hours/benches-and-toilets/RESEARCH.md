# Research: benches-and-toilets

## What footprints and looks should a bench and a toilet block take?

A standard park bench is 1.2 to 1.8 m long. Its seat is about 0.5 m deep and 0.45 m high, and with a backrest it is about 0.9 m deep overall. A bench of 2.0 by 1.0 m, 1.0 m high, is a real bench at the park's scale and stays visible from the camera's usual height. It is far smaller than a shop, so it reads as street furniture.

Real toilet blocks vary widely. Small precast restroom buildings are about 3 by 4 m to 3 by 7 m. Modular blocks with many stalls cover 50 m² and more. A theme park's toilet block serves crowds, so the larger end fits. The milestone asks for about a shop's size, and 8.0 by 5.0 m, 3.5 m high, is a shop's width with less depth. It is lower than a shop, which is 4 m high, so the two read apart at a glance.

The frontage snap catches the pointer on the paving or within the setback plus the box's depth of an edge line. A bench therefore catches within 2 m of the paving's edge, or anywhere on the paving. Pointing at the path to place a bench works, so the short reach is enough.

Colors follow the scene's rule that each kind has its own color. The bench is a wood brown, and the toilet block is a pale teal. Neither is close to the shop's orange red, the depot's blue, the entrance's yellow, the paths' tan and gray, or the starved mark's purple.

Rejected: a bench at its real 0.5 m depth — the frontage catch would shrink to 1.5 m, and a 0.5 m box is hard to see or pick. A toilet block the size of a large modular building, 12 m or more — it would crowd small parks and dwarf the shops that matter more. A toilet block at a shop's exact size and height — the two would be told apart by color alone.
