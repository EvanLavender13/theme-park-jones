# Research: food-overlay

## Which colors should the ramp use?

Viridis sampled at quarters of its range is a standard five-color palette: #440154, #3B528B, #21908C, #5DC863, and #FDE725, dark purple through blue, teal, and green to yellow. Interpolating linearly between neighboring stops in RGB stays close to the continuous map, since viridis itself is built from short, nearly straight runs, and keeps its lightness rising steadily, which is what makes it readable in grayscale and with red-green color deficiency.

Zero means no supplied shop can be reached, which the player must tell apart from a little food. A neutral gray, 0.55 in each channel, is never given by the ramp: along the ramp red reaches 0.55 only between green and yellow, where green is above 0.8. Viridis' lowest stop is a dark purple, so the smallest nonzero value is also far from gray.

The ramp's top is fixed at 0.5, one meal's full relief with no walk and no wait, so the same color means the same availability in every park and at every tick, and a place near two supplied shops saturates at yellow.

Rejected: a ramp scaled to each frame's greatest value. Colors would shift as waits change and would not compare between captures. Rejected: zero as the ramp's lowest color. Nearly nothing and nothing would look the same.

## How high should the band's tent lie?

Guest paths lie 4 cm above the terrain and backstage paths 2 cm, and the band must lie under both, so its line lies at 1.5 cm and its edges at 0.5 cm. The drop of 1 cm over the band's 6 m width makes two lines' surfaces at a point differ by 1 cm for each 6 m of difference in their distances, so they tie only on the line halfway between them, which is the boundary the rule asks for anyway.

The renderer's depth is reversed in a 32-bit float buffer where the device has one, which keeps a relative precision of about 2^-24 at every distance, about 0.02 mm at the camera's 400 m limit. The half centimeter between the band's edge and the terrain, and between its line and the backstage paths, is far above that at every pitch the camera allows.

Rejected: translucent bands blended over the grass. Where bands overlap, whichever is drawn later blends over the other, so the overlap shows a mix, not the nearest line's value. Rejected: one flat height. Overlapping bands at one height tie in depth and flicker.

## Where along a line should the overlay sample?

Availability along an edge is piecewise linear: route distance inside an edge is the lesser of its two ends' distances plus the offset, and offers are sampled at nodes. It kinks only at stops, where the nearer end can change, at the point where the two ends' routes are equally long, and where a shop's effective time crosses a point of the discount curve. Sampling each stop and each point of the line, where the band turns, and every OVERLAY_SPACING, 1 m, between them makes the band exact at nodes and bends, and keeps the interpolation within half a meter of each kink. Each sample costs a route distance sample and one offer sample per reachable source, so a build's cost grows with the total length of guest path, not with the number of edges or how the paths are split. On fed.park's 120 m it is about 130 samples, and warm.park's build measured 2.6 ms on windows-debug. On a park with one guest path about 3 km long (Evan's lowfps.park, one shop), a build is about 3,100 samples and takes about 38 ms on windows-debug, of which about 17 ms is route distance sampling, about 13 ms finding offers, and about 7 ms the mesh; windows-release takes about 2 ms. Rebuilt on every tick, 33 ms apart, the debug build cannot keep up. The next section covers what to do about it.

## What is the purest way to keep the overlay current on a large park?

The overlay's cost has three separable causes, and each matches a problem other fields have studied.

What is computed. Food availability is a gravity accessibility measure: a sum over destinations of each one's weight passed through a decay of travel cost. Accessibility tools such as Conveyal's R5 and Pandana compute travel costs once, from shortest-path searches over the network, and then combine them with destination weights, so changing what destinations offer re-weights a stored cost table instead of routing again. Here the route distance field is that cost table, already computed at resolution, and the per-tick inputs are only each shop's offer: relief, wait, and whether it is supplied. Influence maps in games use the same split: Dave Mark's tactical maps precompute path distances from each source by Dijkstra, or stamp precomputed templates, rather than measuring distance at every cell on every update. Since the wait sits inside the decay curve, and the curve is piecewise linear rather than exponential, a shop's term does not factor into a static part times a dynamic weight. Evaluating it from a stored distance and the current wait still costs only arithmetic.

How it is represented. Sampling every meter is the lixel approach of network kernel density estimation, which splits edges at a fixed resolution and evaluates at each piece, at a cost proportional to the pieces times the sources. That literature, and Mike Lewis's infinite-resolution influence mapping, both note that when the falloff has a known closed form, the field can be evaluated exactly without a fixed grid, at a cost that follows the sources rather than the resolution. Availability along an edge has that form. Route distance there is the lesser of two linear functions of the offset (routes' sampleEdge), and the discount curve is piecewise linear in time, so each shop's term is piecewise linear along the edge with breakpoints computable from the edge's two end distances, the wait, and the curve's points. Rows at those breakpoints and at the line's bends give a band that is exact everywhere, instead of within half a meter of each kink, with a row count that follows edges times shops times curve points, independent of length. For lowfps.park that is on the order of a hundred rows instead of 3,100. Doing so means the overlay relies on route distance's edge rule, which the routes spec and header state publicly. Whether legible may derive breakpoints from that rule, or routes should offer the profile along an edge itself, is a placement question for the plan.

Colors with sparse rows. The overlay colors each vertex and the GPU blends the colors between them. Scientific visualization treats this as a known error: blending colors across a polygon gives colors the map never contains, wherever the map is not linear between the two values. VTK's InterpolateScalarsBeforeMapping fixes it by interpolating the scalar and looking the color up per pixel in a one-dimensional texture. With rows every meter the error is invisible. With rows only at breakpoints, a piece whose value crosses a viridis stop would show a wrong color unless a row is added at each crossing, which is exact and computable, or the value is carried to the fragment shader instead of a color.

When it is recomputed. The overlay is a pure function of the guest network, the route distance field, and the offers of reachable shops. Incremental computation frameworks such as Salsa, Adapton, and the build systems Mokhov, Mitchell, and Peyton Jones classify keep such a derived value with the versions of its inputs and recompute only when an input changes. They also stop early when a recomputed input turns out equal to before. For the overlay that means rebuilding when an edit lands or a candidate is made, and otherwise only when some shop's offer differs from the last build's. An idle park with steady offers then rebuilds nothing, while the overlay still shows every tick's change. This is the design notes' "resolution over time" and shared-medium's change-driven resolution candidate applied to one consumer. Decision 0027 accepts such a cache when one component owns it and one path invalidates it. keepPreview already works this way for the candidate, keyed on tick and edit, and the overlay's key would be its inputs instead of the tick.

Together these suggest the purest solution: an exact piecewise-linear band whose breakpoints come from the field's own form, colored without blending error, and rebuilt only when its inputs change. Each part stands alone. Change-driven rebuilding alone removes the idle cost in this case, but not the cost when a busy shop's wait changes every tick. Exact breakpoints alone cut each build about thirtyfold here and remove the approximation, but still rebuild every tick. Finding each shop's offer once per build, the milestone's deepening candidate, removes about a third of the cost and is compatible with both.

How far it scales. The discount curve reaches zero at 240 s, about 312 m at walking speed less the wait, so each edge's terms come only from the shops within that reach, and an exact band's rows grow with edges times local shop density: linear in park size. Rebuilding the whole overlay whenever any offer changes does not scale the same way, since in a park with many shops some wait changes nearly every tick. The scalable form invalidates only the edges within the changed shop's reach and rebuilds the mesh in chunks, as chunked terrain meshes in games do. Two costs below the overlay grow faster and are not the overlay's to fix. Sampling scans every entry of every source, so reading each shop's distance at an edge's ends costs sources times nodes, which is affordable-sampling's "entries indexed by place" candidate. And route distance runs a search from every source over the whole network at each resolution, storing sources times nodes entries. A candidate pays that on every tick while a ghost is shown, which is world-as-value's incremental resolution candidate. Guests' choice weighs distance linearly with no cutoff, so bounding route distance's reach the way the overlay's curve bounds itself would change guest behavior, a design question rather than an optimization.

Rejected: caching each sample's route distances and recoloring every tick. It is the accessibility tools' split between costs and weights, but it keeps the per-meter approximation, still does work proportional to length on every tick, and adds a cache keyed on the tick rather than on what the overlay depends on. Rejected: rebuilding on a timer, as tactical influence maps do every half second to two seconds. It is cheap, but the overlay would lag the simulation it explains, against principle 8's traceability. Rejected: splitting long paths into more edges. The build's cost follows path length, not edge count, and more nodes make each route sample slightly dearer.
