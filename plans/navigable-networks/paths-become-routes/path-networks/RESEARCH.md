# Research: path-networks

## How are meetings turned into stops and nodes deterministically?

A meeting between two segments names a distance on each of their carriers. Several meetings can land within a hair of each other: a snapped point that also lies at a ground-line point of the other line meets both of that line's segments there, and a crossing right beside a line point is found both as a crossing and as an end within the tolerance. The standard remedy is to snap nearby events together before building the graph. On each carrier, the distances are sorted and grouped greedily from the lowest: a group takes everything within the tolerance of its first member, so every member lies within the tolerance of the stop and stops lie more than the tolerance apart. Each meeting then says that a stop on one carrier and a stop on the other are the same node, and a union-find over stops gives the nodes as the equivalence classes. Greedy grouping and union-find both give a result that depends only on the set of distances and meetings, not on the order the segment pairs were tested in, so long as the node numbers are assigned afterwards by a fixed walk: carriers in key order, stops in distance order.

A carrier may meet itself, where a path crosses itself or loops back through its own earlier stretch. The medium allows one node at two stops of a carrier, and even at two consecutive stops, which gives an edge from a node back to itself. Neighboring segments share a line point and always meet there, so they are skipped. A self-meeting whose two distances fall in one group is the same stop, and joins nothing.

The orientation test decides a proper crossing from the signs of four cross products, and the fractions along each segment come from the same products, so the crossing point needs no division beyond one per segment. Where the two segments do not properly cross, their nearest points include an end of one of them, so testing each of the four ends against the other segment, with the clamped projection the medium's nearestPlaceOn uses, finds every other meeting within the tolerance, including collinear overlaps and lines passing through each other's line points. The end tests run for crossing segments too: a path drawn between points snapped onto a diagonal line lies within rounding of it, so its segments can cross the other line at a shallow angle anywhere along their length, and only the end tests put a node at the snapped points.

Rejected: grouping by chains, each distance joining a group when within the tolerance of the group's last member. A dense run of meetings would then merge into one stop however long it grew. Exact stops with no grouping, which would leave edges a fraction of a millimeter long between near-duplicate nodes. A sweep-line intersection search, which the slice's scale does not need (../RESEARCH.md).
